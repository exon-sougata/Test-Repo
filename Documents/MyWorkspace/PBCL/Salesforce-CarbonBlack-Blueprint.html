S.03Part I — Business Case
WHY THIS MARKET IS DIFFERENT

Carbon black is B2B at its most concentrated and technical

A handful of buyer groups drive most of the volume, every account buys a different mix of grades, and the sales cycle runs through engineering labs before it ever reaches procurement. Generic CRM account management wasn't built for this.

01 · BUYER CONCENTRATION

A small number of groups, a large number of plants

Tire majors and a handful of rubber, plastics, ink and coatings groups typically account for the large majority of offtake — but each negotiates centrally and receives locally, across dozens of manufacturing sites.

02 · GRADE COMPLEXITY

30–40+ ASTM grades, no two accounts alike

The N100–N900 series spans distinct furnace parameters, surface area, structure and pH specs. Two plants in the same group can run entirely different qualified grades and packaging requirements.

03 · FEEDSTOCK VOLATILITY

Pricing is formula-linked, not fixed

Carbon black feedstock oil cost swings drive frequent, index-linked price revisions — commercial terms have to move with cost, account by account, contract by contract.

04 · QUALIFICATION CYCLES

6–18 months before the first PO

New grade or plant qualification runs through technical trials with the customer's R&D and quality teams long before commercial terms are even discussed.

05 · TENDER-DRIVEN VOLUME

Annual and multi-year offtake agreements

Large accounts run structured RFQs or tenders at the group level, then execute against them plant-by-plant for the life of the contract.

06 · MIXED CHANNEL

Direct accounts and distributor-served demand

Smaller compounders and regional buyers are frequently served through distributors — a channel that is commercially important but usually invisible in the CRM.

PART I — THE BUSINESS CASE03
S.04Part I — Business Case
WHAT THIS LOOKS LIKE TODAY

Six gaps a fragmented, spreadsheet-driven process creates

01

No group-level account view

Each plant or subsidiary is tracked as its own record. Nobody can see total spend, relationship health, or contract coverage for the buying group as a whole.

02

Manual, spreadsheet-based quoting

Grade-wise pricing and volume tiers live in Excel — slow to update against feedstock moves, and error-prone when a rep quotes an off-spec grade or the wrong tier.

03

Sales and service don't talk

Quality complaints and certificate-of-analysis issues are logged in a separate system — the account owner is often the last to know a key customer has an open complaint.

04

Distributor demand is a blind spot

Indirect accounts have no digital record of end-use, order pattern or stock position — the channel that reaches the smallest buyers is the one CRM sees least.

05

Forecasting is disconnected from sales

Demand planning and feedstock procurement run on historical shipment data — not on what sales is actually hearing from accounts about upcoming volume.

06

Contract renewals get missed

Multi-year offtake agreements are tracked manually against calendar reminders — renewal and repricing windows are easy to miss until a competitor is already in the account.

S.07Part III — Platform Deep Dive
SALES CLOUD

Account Hierarchy & the 360° View

The foundation everything else in this blueprint sits on top of.

Unlimited-depth parent/child hierarchy

DATA MODEL

Group → regional procurement office → plant, modeled natively in Accounts. Any new subsidiary, plant or legal entity slots in without a redesign.

Roll-up fields at every level

AUTOMATION

Volume, revenue, open cases and contract count roll up from plant to region to group automatically — no manual consolidation, no stale spreadsheet.

Account teams tied to the hierarchy

TERRITORY

A Key Account Manager owns the group relationship; local reps own individual plants — territory and account-team rules enforce who sees and touches what.

One console, every child account

UX

The Lightning Account console surfaces contracts, quotes, orders, cases and activity across every child account from a single screen — no tab-hopping between plant records.

PART III — PLATFORM DEEP DIVE07
S.08Part III — Platform Deep Dive
SALES CLOUD

Opportunity, pipeline & tender management

A stage process that matches how carbon black is actually sold — not a generic B2B pipeline.

Stage-gated to the real cycle

PROCESS

Technical Qualification → RFQ / Tender → Commercial Negotiation → Contract → Won — each stage gated on the evidence that actually moves a carbon black deal forward (trial results, spec sign-off, index terms agreed).

Multi-year offtake as a linked object

CONTRACTS

A won tender becomes an Opportunity linked to a Contract record with its own term, volume commitment and repricing schedule — not a closed deal that disappears from view.

Group-level tender, plant-level execution

HIERARCHY-AWARE

One Opportunity rolled up at the parent account even when individual plants raise their own purchase orders against it — leadership sees the whole tender, not fragments.

Pipeline sliced by grade, region, plant

VISIBILITY

Forecast and pipeline reporting cut by ASTM grade and geography, not just by sales rep — the view production and procurement actually need.

PART III — PLATFORM DEEP DIVE08
S.09Part III — Platform Deep Dive
CPQ

Configure, price, quote — by grade, by contract

Turns grade-and-feedstock pricing from an Excel exercise into a guided, governed quoting flow.

Grade-specific price books

PRODUCT MODEL

Every ASTM grade (N100–N900 series) is a Product with its spec attributes — surface area, structure, pH — attached, so a quote can never leave with the wrong spec.

Volume-tier & index-linked pricing

PRICING RULES

Price rules encode both volume-break discounts and formula links to feedstock cost — a price revision propagates to every affected quote instead of a round of manual re-typing.

Guided selling for technical reps

GUARDRAILS

Configuration rules block incompatible combinations — an unqualified grade for that plant, or packaging/logistics the site can't receive — before the quote ever reaches the customer.

Approvals, e-signature, contract repository

GOVERNANCE

Below-floor pricing and contract redlines route through approval automatically; signed contracts land in the repository attached to the correct account in the hierarchy.

S.10Part III — Platform Deep Dive
SERVICE CLOUD

Quality complaints stop living outside the CRM

The link most manufacturing CRM rollouts miss: closing the loop between what service knows and what sales sees.

Quality-complaint case type

DATA MODEL

Cases extended with batch number, certificate-of-analysis reference and plant of origin — a complaint is traceable to the exact production run, not just "a customer called."

SLAs by contract tier

ENTITLEMENTS

Entitlement rules give key accounts faster guaranteed response — enforced automatically, not dependent on whoever happens to pick up the case.

Closed loop to QC and R&D

ESCALATION

Escalation routes a case to the lab, captures root cause, and tracks the corrective action back to completion — visible on the same account timeline as the commercial relationship.

Complaint trend inside Account 360

VISIBILITY

The account owner sees open and historical complaints on the same screen as the pipeline — a renewal conversation never starts blind to an unresolved quality issue.

EXPERIENCE CLOUD

Closing the distributor blind spot

A self-service portal for the channel that reaches the smallest buyers — the one segment traditional CRM rollouts usually leave out.

Self-service for distributors & smaller accounts

PORTAL

Place orders, track shipments, and download certificates of analysis without a phone call or email to the sales team.

Demand signal flows back into CRM

DATA CAPTURE

Distributor-reported end-customer and stock data becomes structured CRM data — the indirect channel stops being invisible to demand planning.

Lower-value inbound off the sales team's desk

EFFICIENCY

Order-status checks and COA requests — high volume, low value — are handled by the portal, freeing reps for qualification and negotiation work.

S.12Part III — Platform Deep Dive
INTEGRATION LAYER

The Account 360 is only as real as the ERP data feeding it

Sales, quality and production have always run in separate systems here. Integration is what actually makes "360°" true rather than aspirational.

SAP / ERP ↔ Salesforce, via MuleSoft

MIDDLEWARE

Orders, inventory position and production allocation sync into the Account 360 — a rep sees real delivery status without calling the plant.

QC / LIMS batch & COA feed

QUALITY DATA

Batch and certificate-of-analysis data pushes automatically into Service Cloud cases, so a complaint is pre-populated with the actual production evidence.

Logistics & dispatch visibility

FULFILLMENT

Shipment and dispatch status lands on the Account timeline, so account teams and the distributor portal both show the same live answer.

Demand signal flows back out

BI-DIRECTIONAL

What CRM captures about upcoming volume feeds S&OP and feedstock procurement planning — not a one-way integration.

ANALYTICS & EINSTEIN

From account records to a forecasting signal

The same hierarchy that powers the 360° view is what makes group-level analytics possible in the first place.

Volume & revenue dashboards, by grade and region

DASHBOARDS

CRM Analytics / Tableau views cut by ASTM grade, geography and account tier — built on the same rolled-up hierarchy data, not a separate reporting exercise.

Renewal-risk & opportunity scoring

EINSTEIN

Model flags contracts approaching renewal with adverse signals — a complaint spike, a volume dip — before the account team finds out from the customer.

Group-level account health score

SCORING

A single blended score — volume trend, complaint trend, renewal proximity — rolled up to the parent account for leadership review.

Feeds S&OP and feedstock planning

PLANNING

Sales-reported demand becomes an input to demand planning and feedstock procurement — not a number production discovers after the fact.
